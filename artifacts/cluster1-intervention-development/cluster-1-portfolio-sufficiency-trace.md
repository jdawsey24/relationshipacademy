## 4. Proposed disposition (owner ratifies — do not treat as decided)

Reading the rubric against the trace:

- **If the owner upholds the v1.1 FCQ-open re-triage on PE-1** → **PARTIALLY SUFFICIENT.** A *major*
  pathway (PE-1, the 28-statement core) carries an unresolved FCQ; PE-2/PE-4/PE-6 are partial. Evidence
  required = this trace, identifying PE-1 (+ the PE-4 Reciprocity caveat, PE-6 expectancy gap) as the
  uncovered/partial major pathways.
- **If the owner ratifies PE-1 Resolution B (Task-Supporting) as adequate** → **SUFFICIENT WITH
  RECOGNITION/CONTEXT AREAS** (PE-8 documented as intentional Recognition), *provided* the PE-4 Reciprocity
  mapping caveat and the PE-6 expectancy gap are accepted as adequate-with-caveat rather than blocking.

**Most-likely honest landing: Partially Sufficient** — because even ratifying PE-1's Task-Supporting target
leaves PE-4 (weak Reciprocity mapping) and PE-6 (no uncertainty/hope competency) as caveated partials on
non-trivial pathways. That is not a failure — Partially Sufficient clears R5; it just bounds the claim (§5).

## 5. Product-claim scoping (bounds the Playbook's promise; not consumer copy)

Per the Standard's Product-Claim Scoping Principle, the disposition bounds what the Playbook may imply:
- **Adequately covered → may claim to help:** reading relational evidence and deciding (PE-5),
  authentic self-presentation (PE-3), intentional rest vs avoidant withdrawal (PE-7).
- **Partial → claim only the specific addressed operation, with caveat:** investment pacing (PE-4, the
  observable evidence→move, not "reciprocity" as a construct); bounding a conclusion (PE-1 via Bounded
  Reflection, as a *buffer* on the self-verdict — **must not imply it resolves self-worth**); the
  evaluator-stance reorientation (PE-2).
- **Must NOT claim:** to comprehensively resolve the self-worth core (PE-1) or to correct *accurate*
  negative expectancy (PE-6, Context-Validity §9).
- **Recognition/referral (state explicitly):** loneliness (PE-8).
