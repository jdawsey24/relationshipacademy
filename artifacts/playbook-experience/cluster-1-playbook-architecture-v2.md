**Status:** ARCHITECTURE REVISION for review. NOT implemented, NOT final copy, NOT extended to other
clusters. The v1 UX, behavioral derivation, literature concepts, interactions, and approved methodology
are **preserved as source material** (see `cluster-1-difficulty-feeling-chosen-{design,ux-architecture,
content-architecture-and-copy}-v1.md` and `../behavioral-derivation/`). Governing task: **Discernment
(Exploration)**. Experience Cluster: **Difficulty Feeling Chosen** (unchanged).

## A. Architecture Change Summary

**v1 (retired as the product shape, kept as source):** a single **linear 21-screen** experience that ran
every user through **PRESENT → OBSERVE → DECIDE** as three forced primary Plays, then a summary. It
implicitly treated all three behaviors as applying to everyone with the Experience Cluster.

**v2 (new hypothesis):** the Snapshot identifies the *experience*; the Playbook must **not** infer that
everyone with that experience performs the same behaviors. So the Playbook becomes:

```
Experience Cluster  →  Candidate Behavioral Pathways  →  Play Board  →  User Recognition
                    →  Activated Play  →  Behavioral Practice  →  My Plays
```

- A brief **Playbook-level literature** layer explains the Experience Cluster + the Discernment reframe.
- A **Play Board** presents a curated set of **Candidate Behavioral Pathways** as cards — each a
  *recognizable behavioral situation*, not an emotion/outcome/trait/need.
- The user **self-recognizes** into the card(s) that apply ("This sounds like me" / "Not really my pattern").
- A recognized card becomes an **Activated Play** (first-party confirmation) with
  **Recognize → Understand → Practice → Run the Play → Save**.
- Useful Plays are saved to **My Plays** (a collection, not one summary).

**Key reframe:** *Authentic Self-Presentation, Reciprocity-Based Investment, Evidence-Informed
Decision-Making are no longer "three Plays."* They are **behavioral protocols/targets** that power the
adaptive response *inside* Plays. A **Play** is now a recognizable *behavioral situation the user chooses*.

**Nothing is destroyed** — every v1 interaction is repurposed (see G).

## B. Candidate Play Board — evidence-supported cards

Derived from: Cluster 1 Tier-1 evidence, the provisionally-validated Cross-Cluster Rule, the approved
Tier-2 candidates, held/rejected candidates, the Core Play Library, C1 phenomenology, Exploration →
Discernment, and the approved Exploration competencies. **Smallest useful, non-redundant set = 4 cards
(3 primary + 1 context-activated). No pathways were manufactured to fill the board.**

### CARD A — Self-Editing  *(ADMIT · Tier 1)*
- **Internal Pathway Name:** Authentic Self-Presentation / Self-Editing.
- **Proposed Card Concept (plain):** "I change myself to match what I think they want — I hold back a real preference, opinion, or need early on."
- **Evidence Tier:** Tier 1 (direct). **Supporting IDs:** STM-0262. **Source Cluster:** 1. **Phase/Task:** Exploration / Discernment. **Competency:** Emotional Intimacy → Authenticity.
- **Evidence Chain:** C1 phenomenology of earning/proving/changing to be chosen (STM-0152/0153/0260) → Discernment requires accurate information about *both* people → self-editing degrades that information → the pathway is relevant to offer.
- **Relevance to C1:** HIGH. **May ask:** whether they recognize hiding/changing themselves early on. **May NOT assume:** that they do this; or that it stems from low self-worth / a fear (no mechanism).
- **Cluster Specificity:** HIGH · **Assumption Burden:** LOW · **Overlap:** minimal (disclosure, not pursuit/decision).
- **Recommendation: ADMIT.**

### CARD B — Over-Pursuit  *(ADMIT · Tier 2, human-approved)*  ★ prototype Play (Part D)
- **Internal Pathway Name:** Reciprocity-Based Investment / Over-Pursuit.
- **Proposed Card Concept (plain):** "I put in more effort when they give me less — I chase harder when someone pulls back."
- **Evidence Tier:** Tier 2 (governed cross-cluster). **Supporting IDs:** STM-0395, STM-0042 (behavioral); STM-1039 (adaptive observation component). **Source Clusters:** 5 (Exploration); 24 (Exclusivity, translated to *observe consistency*). **Phase/Task:** Exploration / Discernment. **Competencies:** Role Functioning → Reciprocity; Trust → Availability, Congruence.
- **Evidence Chain:** C1 "I care more than they do / feel like an option" (STM-0256/0257) → Discernment requires *observed* reciprocity → over-pursuit fills the relational space so reciprocity is unobservable → pacing effort to reciprocity restores Discernment.
- **Relevance to C1:** HIGH (the being-an-option dynamic). **May ask:** whether they recognize increasing effort when the other gives less. **May NOT assume:** that they do; or that they chase "because they fear not being chosen."
- **Cluster Specificity:** HIGH · **Assumption Burden:** LOW–MODERATE (cross-cluster pathway offered, never attributed) · **Overlap:** distinct.
- **Recommendation: ADMIT.**

### CARD C — Open-Ended Waiting  *(ADMIT · Tier 2, human-approved)*
- **Internal Pathway Name:** Evidence-Informed Decision-Making / Open-Ended Waiting.
- **Proposed Card Concept (plain):** "I keep waiting for them to decide about us — I give another chance and hope it changes, instead of deciding for myself."
- **Evidence Tier:** Tier 2. **Supporting IDs:** STM-0039, STM-0556. **Source Cluster:** 5 (Exploration). **Phase/Task:** Exploration / Discernment. **Competencies:** Role Functioning → Boundaries; Trust → Accountability.
- **Evidence Chain:** C1 "waiting for them to choose me / keep hoping they'll change their mind" (STM-0440/0442) → Discernment requires acting on sufficient evidence → open-ended waiting defers the decision indefinitely → deciding from observed evidence (condition-based, no timeline) restores Discernment.
- **Relevance to C1:** HIGH (waiting to be chosen). **May ask:** whether they recognize open-ended waiting / repeated open-ended chances. **May NOT assume:** that they do; or why.
- **Cluster Specificity:** MODERATE–HIGH (shares territory with C24; the *waiting-to-be-chosen* framing is C1-specific) · **Assumption Burden:** LOW · **Overlap:** distinct.
- **Recommendation: ADMIT.**

### CARD D — Stepping Back From Dating  *(ADMIT as CONTEXT-ACTIVATED · Tier 1)*
- **Internal Pathway Name:** Intentional Dating Break (context-activated).
- **Proposed Card Concept (plain):** "I've pulled back from dating because it's worn me out."
- **Evidence Tier:** Tier 1. **Supporting IDs:** STM-0177. **Source Cluster:** 1. **Phase/Task:** Exploration / Discernment (intentionality preserves agency). **Competency:** Role Functioning → Intentionality.
- **Evidence Chain:** C1 dating fatigue/hopelessness (STM-0172–0184) → stepping back can be *intentional rest* or *avoidance* → distinguishing them (never classifying which) preserves agency and the ability to re-enter Discernment.
- **Relevance to C1:** MODERATE (a real but non-universal dimension). **May ask:** whether dating currently feels draining and they've pulled back. **May NOT assume:** that everyone needs a break; or that their break is avoidant.
- **Cluster Specificity:** MODERATE · **Assumption Burden:** LOW–MODERATE (surfaced only when its context self-check is positive) · **Overlap:** distinct dimension (burnout, not the being-chosen core).
- **Recommendation: ADMIT as CONTEXT-ACTIVATED** — visually distinct, surfaced conditionally, never presented as a step everyone takes.

### Not on the board
- **Don't-dismiss-available people (STM-0396) — HELD** (its center of gravity is partner *selection*, not Difficulty Feeling Chosen; insufficient C1 specificity). Not a card. Precedent preserved: task-relevance alone ≠ admissible.
- **Red-flag checking (STM-0037) — REJECTED** (semantic-only; belongs to self-trust, C5).

## C. Play Board UX

**Feel:** a sophisticated **behavioral strategy deck / library** — *not* a game show. No points, dollar
values, scoring, timers, or competitive mechanics. Jeopardy is referenced only for the *choice*
architecture (multiple visible options; you pick what to explore; explored options change state; you can
return and choose another).

**Flow:** `Playbook literature (brief) → Play Board → open a card → Activated Play → back to board / My Plays`.

**Board framing (representative, not final):** *"Difficulty Feeling Chosen can show up alongside different
responses. Which of these, if any, feels familiar to you?"* — never "because your result is X, you do these."

**Cards:** each shows one plain behavioral situation + a low-pressure affordance to indicate **"This sounds
like me"** or effectively **"This isn't really my pattern."** Recognition is optional; the user is never
required to identify with any card.

**Card states:** `Available` (not yet explored) · `Explored` (opened) · `Added to My Plays` (recognized +
saved). Future: `Used` (voluntary "I tried this"). States are shown with label + shape/icon (never
color-only). No completion %, streaks, badges, scores, or "healing" language.

**Context-activated card (D):** rendered visually distinct and introduced with its own short self-check
(*"does dating feel draining right now?"*); if not relevant, it stays quiet/low-emphasis rather than
pushed. Never implies everyone needs it.

**Return behavior:** the board persists card states across sessions; the user can explore one, several,
return later, decide a card doesn't apply, remove a saved Play, or finish without exploring everything.
The Playbook is a *return-to-when-relevant* resource, not a linear completion.

**Mobile:** vertical stack of tappable cards (the current shell + design tokens); tap opens the Play,
"Back to board" returns. **Accessibility:** cards are labeled buttons with visible focus states; state is
conveyed by text + icon; reduced-motion honored; each Play is keyboard-navigable; no timing pressure.

## D. One Fully Designed Activated Play — **Over-Pursuit** (Card B)

Chosen as the strongest admitted pathway to fully design: highest C1 specificity (the being-an-option
dynamic) **and** it carries the signature **two-lane Reciprocity Timeline** — the strongest interaction
concept from v1. *(Self-Editing (Card A, Tier 1) is the alternative "strongest by evidence tier"; it would
reuse the PRESENT recognition + scenario rehearsal. Say the word to design that one instead.)*

**1 · RECOGNIZE**
- Show the card concept + 2 concrete examples (representative): *"When they take longer to reply, I reply
  faster." "When they seem less interested, I try harder to win them back."*
- Affordances: **"Yes, I do something like this"** → continue · **"Actually, this isn't me"** → exit back
  to the board (no penalty, card returns to `Available`). *This is the first-party confirmation that turns
  a Candidate Pathway into an Activated Play.*

**2 · UNDERSTAND** *(concise; behavioral, no mechanism)*
- What the behavior does: filling every gap leaves no room to see what the other person brings.
- How it affects Discernment: you can't tell if interest is mutual when you're supplying all of it.
- What becomes harder to see: reciprocity, availability, whether their actions match their words.
- What the adaptive alternative accomplishes: pacing effort leaves space so *the pattern of mutual
  participation* becomes observable. **No "why you do this," no attachment/fear/pathology.**

**3 · PRACTICE — the two-lane Reciprocity Timeline** *(preserved v1 signature)*
- Pass 1 (over-pursuit → "no room to see") → Pass 2 (leaving space → the other lane contributes in
  **differently-shaped, non-mirrored** forms; sometimes nothing → "information too").
- Preserved verbatim: **pattern-level reciprocity** ("their part can look different from yours… not a
  perfect match") and **"Reciprocity is information — not a scoreboard."**

**4 · RUN THE PLAY** *(concrete real-life instruction)*
- Representative: *"When you notice you're giving more as they give less, keep showing interest naturally
  but leave a little space — then watch whether they reach back, follow through, or make time."*

**5 · SAVE → My Plays** *(concise reference, not a journal)*
- WHEN: you notice you're giving more as they give less · MY OLD MOVE: chase harder / fill every gap ·
  THE PLAY: keep showing interest, leave space, watch what they bring · WATCH FOR: reciprocity — do they
  reach back, follow through, make time? · REMEMBER: reciprocity is information, not a scoreboard.
- No interpretation of *why* the old move happens.

## E. Literature Architecture  *(~1,000–1,500 words total across the whole Playbook — a guideline)*

**Playbook-level (before the board, ~400–550 words):** (1) what Difficulty Feeling Chosen describes;
(2) why wanting reciprocal selection is normal; (3) how becoming overly oriented toward "Am I being
chosen?" can narrow Discernment; (4) the RLC shift toward mutual evaluation; (5) that people respond to
this experience in **different ways** (setting up self-recognition, not prescription). Not a lesson.

**Play-level (per Activated Play, ~150–250 words):** just enough for that Play's practice to make sense —
the UNDERSTAND section. For the Over-Pursuit prototype Play, the four UNDERSTAND bullets above are the
skeleton. Do **not** repeat the full Experience-Cluster explanation inside each Play. Avoid course-like depth.

## F. My Plays Architecture

Replaces the single "My Relationship Play." **My Plays** is the user's saved collection of **Activated
Plays** — each concise and real-life-usable:

```
WHEN       the behavioral situation / trigger
MY OLD MOVE the behavior the user recognized (their words / the card)
THE PLAY   the adaptive response
WATCH FOR  the relational information this Play helps reveal
REMEMBER   one short governing principle
```
No psychological interpretation of *why* the Old Move happens. Plays are addable/removable; the collection
is returnable when a situation becomes relevant. Not a journal, not a log of a relationship.

## H. Methodology Impact

**New formal distinction (record in Decision Log):**
- **Candidate Behavioral Pathway** — a behavior supported strongly enough (Tier 1, governed Tier 2, or
  approved Tier 3) to *present to the user for self-recognition*. Means "relevant enough to ask whether it
  applies," **not** "people with this Cluster do this."
- **Activated Play** — a Candidate Pathway the user *confirms applies* and chooses to work with. This is
  **first-party behavioral confirmation.**

**Cross-Cluster Behavioral Evidence Rule — NO modification required (do not alter without approval).** The
rule governs **admissibility** (what may appear on the board). The Candidate→Activated distinction adds a
**downstream, first-party gate** (user recognition) that does not change any admissibility criterion — it
**operationalizes** the rule's absolute prevalence guardrail: the system *offers* an admissible pathway,
the *user* confirms it applies. This strengthens the rule's "admissibility ≠ prevalence / admissibility ≠
inclusion" principle by moving inclusion to the user. The rule's status remains **Provisionally Validated —
Cluster 1 Prototype.**

## I. MVP Recommendation

**MVP (minimum viable revised Playbook):**
- Playbook-level literature (the 5 points, concise).
- The **4-card Play Board** with states `Available / Explored / Added to My Plays` (Card D context-activated).
- **All three primary Plays functional end-to-end** (Recognize → Understand → Practice → Run → Save) —
  feasible because their PRACTICE interactions **already exist** in the v1 prototype (Reciprocity Timeline,
  DECIDE Builder, scenario rehearsal). This is what justifies $29 without inflating card count.
- **My Plays** (save / return / remove).

**v1.1 (defer):** the `Used` state + a Recognize→Practice→Use surface; Card D's fuller build; richer
scenario variety; print/share of My Plays.

**Do NOT build:** points/streaks/badges/scores/health-%/completion pressure; partner tracking (journal +
prevalence risk); AI chat; a fourth manufactured Play; any card not evidence-traced.
