## T1a — Bounded Reflection *(Family 1; PE-1; Task-Supporting)*
- **Statement coverage:** PE-1 global self-verdict + self-blame ("maybe I'm too X") + (pattern branch)
  repeated-outcome overgeneralization. **Functional interference:** a relational outcome collapses into a
  global self-conclusion that forecloses fit-evaluation. **Change target:** bound the self-inference to what
  the evidence establishes. **Competency/status:** Task-Supporting (contributing: Objectivity, Reflection,
  Authenticity). **Mechanism:** M1 construal/perspective shift + M3 global→specific. **Active ingredient:**
  *constraining the scope of a self-inference to exactly what the specific relational evidence supports, while
  preserving the emotion.* **Operation(s):** O1 bound (single event); O2 bound (pattern branch); O3 hold pain
  without converting to identity. **Evidence:** self-distanced reflection (Grossmann & Kross 2014) +
  attribution re-scoping (global→specific ONLY; controllability excluded). **Strength:** Empirically-Supported
  Technique (reasoning) / RLC-Theory-Derived Application. **Routing:** **R1**; **R3** if entrenched (0144,
  0156).
- **Use this when:** a specific relational event begins turning into a conclusion about your overall worth,
  lovability, or future — or a repeated experience turns into "this always happens because something's wrong
  with me."
- **Do NOT use when:** the conclusion is a stable, entrenched, chronic self-belief (→R3); to dispute a real
  safety signal; to argue yourself out of a feeling.
- **Prerequisites:** optional SP-01 buffer if self-criticism blocks the sort.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) name the **event** (one specific thing, or — pattern branch — the specific
  recurrence: "the last three people I pursued became distant"). (2) write **what I'm concluding about myself**
  ("I'm not enough"). (3) write **what this event actually establishes** (specific/temporal: "this person
  didn't want to continue"). (4) write **what it does NOT establish** ("that no one will; that I'm unlovable;
  that something is globally wrong with me"). (5) write the **narrowest defensible conclusion.** *(Pattern
  branch adds:* (3b) *is the recurrence real, and does it point to a* specific, non-global *observation worth
  attending to?* — if yes, **route to T2a** to treat it as evidence, not a self-verdict.)
- **Executable output:** a written **bounded conclusion** (+ optional specific observation routed to T2a).
- **Worked example:** *Before* — After a third date, Priya is told "I don't think we're a match"; she concludes
  "I'm fundamentally not relationship material." *Technique* — event: "one person, after three dates, said not a
  match"; establishes: "we weren't a mutual fit for them"; does NOT establish: "I'm not relationship material /
  no one will choose me"; narrowest: "this particular match didn't work; I don't yet have evidence about the
  next one." *Output:* that bounded sentence. *Application:* she keeps dating without carrying "I'm defective"
  into the next conversation — while still feeling the sting.
- **Successful performance:** produces a conclusion that is (a) specific/temporal, (b) evidence-supported, (c)
  still names the feeling — **not** a positive spin. **NOT** success: "feels better," "their loss," "I'm
  great."
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *positive thinking / "their loss"* → correction: the output must be
  the **narrowest** conclusion, not a flattering one; keep the hurt. *Dismissing a real pattern* (pattern
  branch) → correction: preserve the recurrence as a **specific observation** and route it to T2a; only remove
  the **global self-cause.** *Disputing the emotion* → correction: step 2 records the feeling; you bound the
  *conclusion*, not the feeling.
- **Portable real-world version:** *"What does this actually establish — and what does it not?"* (two
  questions).
- **Real-world transfer cue:** the moment a rejection/outcome starts becoming a sentence about *you*.
- **Learning/repetition:** ~2–3 guided runs to learn; use per triggering event; generalize via the 2-question
  portable form. **Reinforcement:** SP-01.
- **Signs of independent use:** catches the global-conclusion closer to when it happens; produces the bounded
  sentence unprompted; can name what evidence would/would not change it.
- **Safety/contraindications:** entrenched/chronic worth beliefs, trauma-linked collapse, suicidality → R3/
  referral. **Expected functional change:** global self-inferences become more **bounded**; outcomes stop
  overwriting fit-evaluation. **May remain:** disappointment, rejection pain, wanting to be chosen.
- **Relationship to others:** feeds Family 2 (a bounded stance lets you read rejection-evidence without
  collapsing); pattern branch routes to T2a. **Redundancy test:** absorbs T1b (below). **Limitations:**
  reasoning-level evidence; dating transfer untested.

## T1b — Global-to-Specific Attribution *(specified, then CONSOLIDATED into T1a)*

## T1c — Mutual-Fit / Evaluator Stance *(Family 1; PE-2)*
- **Statement coverage:** PE-2 chosenness-organized evaluation. **Functional interference:** attention/self-eval
  organized around "am I being chosen?" displaces evaluating mutual fit. **Change target:** shift from applicant
  stance to mutual-evaluator stance. **Competency:** Role Awareness, Objectivity, Curiosity. **Mechanism:** M1 +
  attention redirection. **Active ingredient:** *directing evaluative attention toward whether the relationship
  fits one's own relational criteria, using one's own observed experience as data — rather than monitoring
  whether one is being selected.* **Operation(s):** O4 identify own criteria/notice own reactions; O5 evaluate
  the other's behavior for fit. **Evidence:** construal/attention (adjacent). **Strength:** ES-Technique/RLC-
  Theory. **Routing:** **R1.**
- **Use this when:** you notice you're mostly tracking whether they like you, not whether this works for you.
- **Do NOT use when:** it would become red-flag hunting, ranking, or perfectionistic screening; when safety
  signals need action, not evaluation.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) name **2–3 things that actually matter to you** in a relationship *right now*
  (not a checklist of the ideal partner — your current relational criteria, e.g., "feeling relaxed being
  myself," "consistent follow-through"). (2) write **what you've actually observed** about the person on those
  dimensions. (3) write **how you feel/react** around them (your own experience is data: energized? guarded?
  small?). (4) **compare** observed + your experience against what matters → mark each: **fit / mismatch /
  not-enough-info.** (5) name **one thing you want to learn next** (→ often routes to T2a/T3a).
- **Executable output:** a short **fit/mismatch/unknown map** on your own criteria + one next-learning item.
- **Worked example:** *Before* — Marcus spends every date performing and afterward only asks "did they like
  me?" *Technique* — matters-to-me: "can I relax," "do they follow through," "curiosity about me"; observed:
  "they follow through on plans (fit); rarely ask about my life (not-enough-info/possible mismatch)"; my
  experience: "I feel on-edge, managing impressions (mismatch signal)"; next: "notice whether they ask
  anything about me next time." *Output:* that map. *Application:* he evaluates the relationship, not just his
  performance — and has a concrete thing to watch.
- **Successful performance:** produces criteria that are **his own** + a fit map that includes **his own
  reactions** as data + a specific next-learning item — **not** a verdict on the other person's overall worth
  and **not** "did they choose me."
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *perfectionistic screening / red-flag hunting* → correction: 2–3
  current criteria, not an ideal-partner checklist; "not-enough-info" is a valid mark. *Just re-asserting "I'm
  choosing too" without evaluating* → correction: you must produce the **observed + experienced** columns, not
  a slogan.
- **Portable version:** *"What matters to me here, what have I actually seen, and how do I feel around them?"*
- **Transfer cue:** during/after time together when you catch yourself auditioning.
- **Learning/repetition:** 2–3 guided; use after dates; generalize via the 3-question portable form.
  **Reinforcement:** reuse across people. **Signs of independent use:** spontaneously tracks own criteria/
  reactions; "did they like me?" becomes one input among several.
- **Safety:** if evaluation surfaces coercion/abuse → route out. **Expected functional change:** selection
  becomes **less central**; mutual-fit evaluation becomes active. **May remain:** wanting to be liked/chosen.
  **Relationship to others:** hands off to T2a (evidence) and T3a (authentic data). **Redundancy test:**
  distinct from T1a (builds evaluation vs bounds a verdict) — **retain.** **Limitations:** little direct dating
  evidence.

## T2a — Evidence Discrimination *(Family 2; PE-5 base; +feeds PE-4/PE-6)*
- **Coverage:** PE-5 reading; feeds PE-4/PE-6-distorted. **Interference:** observations auto-convert into
  interpretations; can't tell what's known vs assumed. **Change target:** sort relational evidence.
  **Competency:** Objectivity, Curiosity, Clarification. **Mechanism:** M7. **Active ingredient:** *separating
  what was directly observed from what was inferred and what is unknown, and identifying the specific
  information that would actually reduce the relevant uncertainty.* **Operation(s):** O7 differentiate; O8
  identify informative next evidence; O9 update. **Strength:** ES-Technique/RLC-Theory. **Routing:** **R1–R2.**
- **Use this when:** after ambiguous communication, or when you're spinning a story from a small event.
- **Do NOT use when:** evidence of danger/serious boundary violation is already sufficient — do **not** "gather
  more" (route to safety); when it would become endless information-gathering.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) name the **relational question** ("are we heading somewhere / do they want
  this too?"). (2) list under **OBSERVED** only what directly happened (their words/actions/timing — quote if
  possible). (3) list under **INTERPRETED** what you think it means (label these as *your* inferences). (4)
  list under **UNKNOWN** what the current evidence can't establish. (5) write **INFORMATIVE NEXT EVIDENCE:**
  one thing that would actually move an item from Unknown/Interpreted toward Observed. (6) **sufficiency
  check:** is there already enough to answer the question? If yes → decide (T2b); if genuinely not → gather the
  *one* informative thing (not everything).
- **Executable output:** an **O/I/U sort** + one **evidence question** (or a "sufficient — decide now" flag).
- **Worked example:** *Before* — Sam gets shorter replies and concludes "they're losing interest, I did
  something wrong." *Technique* — question: "is their interest changing?"; OBSERVED: "replies went from
  paragraphs to one line over 4 days; still initiated one plan"; INTERPRETED: "they're pulling away / it's my
  fault"; UNKNOWN: "whether it's about us, their week, or their style"; next evidence: "how they are on the
  plan they initiated." *Output:* the sort + that evidence question. *Application:* Sam stops acting on the
  story and watches the actual next data point.
- **Successful performance:** can state observed vs inferred vs unknown for the situation + name one genuinely
  informative next evidence — **not** "I feel calmer." **Sufficiency** is addressed (not endless).
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *rationalizing away clear evidence* → correction: repeated,
  observed behavior **is** OBSERVED data, not "unknown"; the sort is not for explaining behavior away. *"Unknown"
  becomes indefinite avoidance* → correction: the sufficiency check forces a decide-or-gather-one-thing call.
- **Portable version:** *"What did I actually see, what am I assuming, what don't I know, and what would tell
  me?"*
- **Transfer cue:** ambiguous text/behavior; the urge to conclude from thin data.
- **Learning/repetition:** 2–3 guided; after any ambiguous interaction; portable 4-question form.
  **Reinforcement:** post-date evidence-update practice. **Signs of independent use:** narrates O/I/U
  spontaneously; catches inferences-as-facts.
- **Safety:** established danger → don't gather, route out. **Expected functional change:** observations are
  **less automatically converted** into unsupported interpretations; uncertainty is less likely to force
  premature conclusions. **May remain:** uncertainty itself. **Relationship:** prerequisite to T2b/T2c/T2d;
  receives T1a's pattern observation. **Redundancy test:** distinct (sort vs decide) — **retain.**
  **Limitations:** debiasing transfer modest.

## T2b — Prospective Decision Rule *(Family 2; PE-5)*
- **Coverage:** PE-5 deciding; "when do I walk away / am I wasting time." **Interference:** open-ended waiting;
  no criterion → indefinite investment or premature exit. **Change target:** convert a relational criterion
  into an evidence-conditioned action rule **before** more investment. **Competency:** Intentionality,
  Boundaries. **Mechanism:** M6 (criterion-dependent). **Active ingredient:** *specifying, in advance, the
  observable condition under which you will take a particular relational action — so the decision tracks
  evidence rather than hope or fear.* **Operation(s):** O10 set criterion; O11 translate to if-then; O12
  recognize indefinite waiting. **Strength:** Empirically-Supported (implementation intentions, d≈0.65)/RLC-
  Theory. **Routing:** **R1–R2.**
- **Use this when:** you're waiting for someone to decide about you, or unsure whether to keep investing.
- **Do NOT use when:** it would become an arbitrary ultimatum/deadline, or an attempt to control the other
  person; when safety already dictates the decision.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) take the **criterion** from T2a/T1c (what matters + what you need to see). (2)
  write **IF [specific observable condition/evidence], THEN I will [specific relational action]** (actions:
  continue observing / clarify directly / maintain / increase / reduce investment / decline / revisit). (3)
  **control-check:** the condition must be about **your action in response to observable evidence**, not about
  making them behave a certain way. (4) set a **review trigger** (an event, not a date: "after the next time
  plans are made or not").
- **Executable output:** a written **if-then decision rule** tied to an observable condition + a review
  trigger.
- **Worked example:** *Before* — Dana keeps hoping the person they're seeing will "decide they want something
  real," and waits indefinitely. *Technique* — criterion (from T1c): "consistent initiation + follow-through";
  rule: "IF over the next few interactions they continue to not initiate and cancel plans, THEN I will clarify
  once directly; if it stays one-sided, I will reduce my investment"; control-check: it's about Dana's response,
  not forcing initiation; review trigger: "next planned/unplanned interaction." *Output:* that rule.
  *Application:* Dana stops open-ended waiting; the decision now tracks observable behavior.
- **Successful performance:** the rule has a **concrete observable cue** + a **specific action** + is about
  the user's response — **not** a deadline, not a demand. Recognizes when "gather more" has become waiting.
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *arbitrary timeline/ultimatum* → correction: condition must be
  observable **evidence**, not a countdown. *Rule to control them* ("if they don't text daily then…") →
  correction: rewrite so the THEN is your investment response to what you observe. *Rigidity* → correction:
  rules can be revised when evidence changes (that's T2a updating).
- **Portable version:** *"If I see ___, then I'll ___."*
- **Transfer cue:** before increasing investment; when stuck waiting.
- **Learning/repetition:** 2–3 guided; before investment decisions; portable if-then. **Reinforcement:** revisit
  at review triggers. **Signs of independent use:** forms if-then rules spontaneously; can explain what
  evidence changed a decision.
- **Safety:** if a rule would prolong exposure to danger → don't; act on safety. **Expected functional change:**
  investment/continuation **responds to evidence**; open-ended waiting decreases. **May remain:** uncertainty,
  hope. **Relationship:** requires T2a first; used by T2c. **Redundancy test:** distinct from T2a (decide vs
  read) — **retain.** **Limitations:** relationship-decision transfer untested.

## T2c — Forward-Value Investment Pacing *(Family 2; PE-4)*
- **Coverage:** PE-4 over-investment. **Interference:** investment driven by what's already given (sunk cost) /
  over-pursuit fills the space so reciprocity is unobservable. **Change target:** set the *next* investment
  level from forward value/current evidence, not prior investment. **Competency:** Objectivity + Intentionality
  (Reciprocity contributing, canonical sense). **Mechanism:** M5 forward-value + M7/M6. **Active ingredient:**
  *evaluating what additional investment is warranted by what the relationship currently demonstrates —
  explicitly setting aside what has already been invested — and leaving enough relational space for reciprocity
  to be observable.* **Operation(s):** O13 separate sunk from forward value; O14 leave space to observe.
  **Strength:** ES-Technique (sunk-cost effect real; correction-efficacy on relationships unproven)/RLC-Theory.
  **Routing:** **R1–R2.**
- **Use this when:** you notice you're giving more as they give less, or "I've already put so much in."
- **Do NOT use when:** it would become scorekeeping/tit-for-tat; when safety dictates the decision.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) name your current **investment** (time/emotional energy/availability/planning/
  exclusivity/practical effort). (2) write **SUNK:** what you've already given (label it as *not* a reason to
  continue). (3) write **CURRENT EVIDENCE:** what the relationship demonstrates now (from T2a; incl. observed
  reciprocity via SP-02). (4) write **FORWARD VALUE:** given only the current evidence, what additional
  investment makes sense? (5) **next investment decision:** one concrete step (maintain / increase / reduce /
  pause / clarify) — often expressed as a T2b if-then. (6) **space check:** am I leaving room to see what they
  bring, or filling every gap?
- **Executable output:** a **next-investment decision** justified by forward value (not sunk cost) + a space
  check.
- **Worked example:** *Before* — Alex plans every date, texts first every time, and thinks "I've invested six
  weeks, I can't stop now." *Technique* — investment: planning + initiation + emotional energy; SUNK: "six
  weeks" (not a reason); current evidence: "they rarely initiate; warm when we meet; no follow-through on
  plans they suggest"; forward value: "current evidence supports *maintaining, not increasing*, and creating
  space"; decision: "I'll stop initiating every time and see whether they reach back (T2b: if no initiation
  over the next stretch, reduce)"; space check: "I've been filling every gap." *Output:* that decision.
  *Application:* Alex paces to evidence and can finally observe reciprocity.
- **Successful performance:** the decision is justified by **forward value/current evidence**, sunk cost is
  explicitly set aside, and there's a **space** element — **not** effort-matching, not "they owe me."
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *scorekeeping/tit-for-tat/text-counting* → correction: the output is
  *your* investment level from evidence, never mirroring their behavior unit-for-unit. *Using it to justify
  leaving/punishing* → correction: forward value can support *maintaining* too; it's a pacing tool, not an exit
  script.
- **Portable version:** *"Given only what I see now — not what I've already put in — what's the right next
  level of investment, and am I leaving space to see them?"*
- **Transfer cue:** before increasing investment; when "I've already given so much."
- **Learning/repetition:** 2–3 guided; at investment decisions; portable form. **Reinforcement:** SP-02
  reciprocity-observation. **Signs of independent use:** separates sunk from forward value spontaneously; stops
  filling every gap.
- **Safety:** — **Expected functional change:** investment becomes **responsive to current evidence**;
  over-pursuit decreases; reciprocity becomes observable. **May remain:** caring a lot, attaching. **Relationship:**
  composes T2a+T2b + SP-02. **Redundancy test:** distinct operation (sunk-vs-forward) — **retain.**
  **Limitations:** no evidence sunk-cost correction improves relationship decisions.

## T2d — Expectation Calibration *(Family 2; PE-6 distorted subset)*  ⚑ weakest evidence
- **Coverage:** PE-6 distorted expectancy ("I expect disappointment," "wait for the other shoe," "don't trust
  when it's going well"). **Interference:** an expectation that **exceeds the evidence** pre-discounts
  disconfirming data. **Change target:** make the expectation proportional to available evidence.
  **Competency:** Objectivity + Curiosity. **Mechanism:** M7 (applied to expectations). **Active ingredient:**
  *comparing a prediction against the evidence for and against it, and adjusting the strength/direction of the
  expectation to match what the evidence currently supports — without requiring optimism.* **Operation(s):**
  O15 calibrate. **Strength:** ⚑ **weakest** — ES mechanism, technique under-evidenced/RLC-Theory. **Routing:**
  **R1–R2.**
- **Use this when:** you catch yourself sure it'll go wrong / discounting good signs — **and** you're not sure
  the expectation is evidence-based.
- **Do NOT use when:** the negative expectation is **supported by repeated observable behavior** (that's
  accurate → Recognition/Context, act on it) — the technique must **never** override accurate perception; when
  safety concerns are real.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) write the **prediction** ("this will fall apart like always"). (2)
  **evidence FOR** it (specific, observed — *about this person/situation*, not the past in general). (3)
  **evidence AGAINST/inconsistent** with it (specific, observed). (4) **UNKNOWN.** (5) **accuracy fork:** if
  the FOR column is strong and specific → the expectation may be **accurate** (route to Recognition/T2b action,
  do not "calibrate away"); if the prediction rests mainly on the **past/generalization** with little
  current-specific support → write a **calibrated expectation** proportional to the current evidence (which may
  still be cautious/uncertain — not hopeful).
- **Executable output:** either a **calibrated expectation** (proportional, possibly still negative/uncertain)
  **or** a flag that "this expectation is evidence-supported — act on it."
- **Worked example:** *Before* — After good early dates, Jordan feels sure "they'll lose interest, they always
  do," and starts withdrawing pre-emptively. *Technique* — prediction: "they'll lose interest"; FOR (current,
  specific): "none yet — they've initiated twice and followed through"; AGAINST: "consistent contact, made
  future plans"; UNKNOWN: "long-term interest"; fork: prediction rests on **past pattern**, not current
  evidence → calibrated expectation: "I don't currently have evidence they're losing interest; it's early and
  uncertain, and I'll keep watching the actual signals." *Output:* that calibrated expectation. *Application:*
  Jordan stops pre-emptively withdrawing based on a prediction the current evidence doesn't support — while
  staying appropriately uncertain.
- **Successful performance:** the expectation is **matched to current, specific evidence**; accurate negative
  expectations are **preserved and acted on**, not argued away; no forced hope. **NOT** success: "I feel
  hopeful now."
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *forced optimism / "give everyone a chance"* → correction: a
  calibrated expectation can remain negative or uncertain; the target is *proportional to evidence*, not
  positive. *Calibrating away a real pattern* → correction: the accuracy fork routes evidence-supported
  expectations to **action**, not calibration.
- **Portable version:** *"Is this expectation coming from what I've actually seen here, or mostly from the
  past? What does the current evidence support?"*
- **Transfer cue:** when a strong negative prediction fires, especially when things are going well.
- **Learning/repetition:** 2–3 guided; when pre-emptive predictions fire; portable 2-question form.
  **Reinforcement:** post-date evidence-update. **Signs of independent use:** distinguishes past-driven from
  evidence-driven predictions; keeps accurate caution.
- **Safety:** never override accurate danger/mistreatment expectations. **Expected functional change:**
  expectations **track evidence**; disconfirming data is less pre-discounted. **May remain:** caution, fear,
  uncertainty. **Relationship:** an application of T2a to expectations; accurate subset → Recognition (RC-02).
  **Redundancy test:** distinct object (prediction vs present observation) — **retain, flagged weakest.**
  **Limitations:** ⚑ technique evidence thin; strongest candidate for future validation or R2.

## T3a — Authentic-Presentation Experiment *(Family 3; PE-3)*
- **Coverage:** PE-3 self-editing. **Interference:** editing/performing so fit is assessed on a curated self.
  **Change target:** express authentically and read the response as fit information. **Competency:** Authenticity,
  Gradual Self-Disclosure, Transparency. **Mechanism:** M4/M8 behavioural experiment. **Active ingredient:**
  *making a specific prediction about the consequence of an authentic expression, performing it in a safe/
  low-risk way, and comparing the actual response to the prediction — so the fear is tested against reality and
  the response becomes fit data.* **Operation(s):** O6 test/express. **Evidence:** behavioural experiments
  (McManus, Van Doorn & Yiend 2012 — both TR & BE beat control, **small** BE advantage). **Strength:**
  ES-Technique/RLC-Theory. **Routing:** **R1 (low-risk + safe context) / R2 (greater complexity) / Excluded
  (unsafe/coercive).**
- **Use this when:** before expressing something you would normally edit/hide to keep someone's interest.
- **Do NOT use when:** coercion/abuse/intimidation/stalking/threats or meaningful relational danger — **Excluded/
  referral**; do not use to force premature deep disclosure.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) pick **one low-risk authentic expression** (graded variant: preference /
  opinion / interest / reasonable disagreement / small boundary / small need). (2) **predict** what will happen
  if you express it honestly (write it first). (3) **safety/risk check:** is this low-risk and the context
  safe for self-guided experimentation? (if no → don't / route). (4) **express it** (unchanged to secure
  approval). (5) **observe** the actual response. (6) **compare** prediction vs outcome and **learn:** about
  the prediction, your ability to present authentically, and the fit — *the response is fit information, not a
  verdict on your worth.*
- **Executable output:** a completed **prediction → expression → observed response → bounded learning** record.
- **Worked example:** *Before* — On dates, Kai always agrees with the other person's plans and hides that they
  prefer quiet evenings, fearing "if I say what I want, they'll lose interest." *Technique* — expression: "I'd
  actually love a low-key evening instead of the loud bar"; prediction: "they'll be annoyed/less interested";
  safety check: low-risk, safe; express it; observed: "they said 'oh thank god, me too' and suggested a
  cafe" — OR, in another run, "they insisted on the bar and seemed put out." *Output:* the record + learning:
  "expressing a preference didn't end it; and how they respond to my preferences is fit data." *Application:*
  Kai stops auto-editing and reads responses as fit information (either outcome is usable).
- **Successful performance:** predicted **before** acting; expressed the **unedited** version; **observed** the
  response; drew a **bounded** learning — regardless of whether the response was warm. **NOT** success: "they
  liked it."
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *unfiltered/oversharing* → correction: graded, low-risk, one item;
  not a confession. *Skipping the prediction* → correction: the prediction-before-action **is** the mechanism —
  without it, it's just talking. *Reading a cool response as "I'm unworthy"* → correction: route that
  conclusion to T1a; the response is **fit** data.
- **Portable version:** *"Predict → say the real thing (small, safe) → watch what actually happens."*
- **Transfer cue:** the moment before you edit yourself to keep someone interested.
- **Learning/repetition:** 1–2 guided + graded real-world variants; use when the edit-urge arises.
  **Reinforcement:** graded-variant repetition. **Signs of independent use:** predicts-then-expresses
  spontaneously; treats responses as fit data.
- **Safety:** unsafe/coercive context → Excluded/referral. **Expected functional change:** self-editing becomes
  **less necessary** for relational participation; fit is read on real information. **May remain:** nervousness
  about being authentic. **Relationship:** disconfirms the fear T1a bounds; produces data for T1c/T2a.
  **Redundancy test:** unique mechanism — **retain (one technique + variants).** **Limitations:** self-guided
  BE evidence thin; safety-gating essential.

## T4a — Intentional Engagement *(Family 4; PE-7)*
- **Coverage:** PE-7 discouragement-governed (dis)engagement. **Interference:** discouragement automatically
  drives withdrawal or continued engagement. **Change target:** make engagement/rest/re-entry intentional.
  **Competency:** Intentionality. **Mechanism:** M6 values-guided implementation intention. **Active
  ingredient:** *converting an automatic, discouragement-driven (dis)engagement into a deliberate choice tied
  to what the person wants engagement or rest to serve, with a condition-based re-entry.* **Operation(s):** O16
  distinguish intentional rest from automatic withdrawal; O17 set condition-based re-entry. **Evidence:**
  implementation intentions + values. **Strength:** ES-Technique/RLC-Theory. **Routing:** **R1–R2; R3 if
  suspected clinical depression.**
- **Use this when:** discouragement/burnout creates an urge to quit dating automatically (or to keep grinding).
- **Do NOT use when:** withdrawal reflects possible clinical depression/anhedonia → R3; do not use to pressure
  re-entry.
- **Full guided procedure:** (1) name the current state: is my (dis)engagement a **choice** or **automatic**
  ("I've stopped without deciding, discouragement is running it")? (2) name what I want **engagement OR rest**
  to *serve* right now (values: recovery? protecting energy? staying open?). (3) **decide** intentionally:
  continue / modify how I engage / pause / rest / reassess. (4) if resting: *what am I stepping away from ·
  what is the break for · what will I do during it · what would tell me I'm ready to reassess* (**duration
  optional**). (5) set a **condition-based re-entry** intention ("IF I notice I want connection again and feel
  recovered, THEN I'll re-enter in a small way").
- **Executable output:** an **engagement/rest/re-entry decision** with (if resting) a purpose + condition-based
  re-entry — **not** a fixed-duration break.
- **Worked example:** *Before* — After months of disappointing dates, Rae stops opening the apps, feeling vaguely
  defeated, without deciding to. *Technique* — state: "automatic — I just went quiet"; what rest would serve:
  "recovering, not punishing myself"; decision: "an intentional pause"; break shape: "stepping away from apps
  (not from friends); for recovery; I'll do things I enjoy; readiness signal = curiosity about dating returns,
  not just loneliness"; re-entry: "IF curiosity returns and I feel recovered, THEN I'll re-enter lightly."
  *Output:* that decision. *Application:* Rae's pause is now a chosen, purposeful rest with a re-entry
  condition — not a defeat-driven disappearance.
- **Successful performance:** names choice-vs-automatic; names a value; sets a condition-based re-entry;
  duration left open — **not** "I quit" by default, **not** a forced 30-day plan.
- **Common misapplications → Correction:** *avoidance disguised as intentionality* → correction: name a
  **readiness signal**; if the "rest" has no re-entry condition and is driven by "it hurts less to hide,"
  that's avoidance — revisit values. *Moralizing rest as failure* → correction: rest is a legitimate intentional
  choice. *Pressured re-entry* → correction: re-entry is condition-based, not scheduled.
- **Portable version:** *"Is this a choice or automatic? What do I want rest/engagement to serve? What's my
  re-entry signal?"*
- **Transfer cue:** the urge to quit dating (or grind on) driven by discouragement.
- **Learning/repetition:** 1–2 guided; when discouragement spikes; portable 3-question form. **Reinforcement:**
  re-entry check. **Signs of independent use:** names choice-vs-automatic spontaneously; rests/re-enters on
  condition, not mood.
- **Safety:** suspected clinical depression → R3. **Expected functional change:** discouragement becomes
  **less likely to automatically determine** engagement; engagement/rest/re-entry are intentional. **May
  remain:** tiredness, discouragement, needing rest. **Relationship:** largely independent/gating.
  **Redundancy test:** unique — **retain.** **Limitations:** BA evidence is clinical; non-clinical transfer
  inferential.
