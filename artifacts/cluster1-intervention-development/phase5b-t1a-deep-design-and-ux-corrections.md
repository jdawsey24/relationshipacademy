## DELIVERABLE 7 — Selected Interaction + Rationale
**Selected: Option A — Evidence Boundary**, scaffolded by **B's narrowing dimensions** and **C's "what would
this take to be true?"** as optional prompts. Rationale: the frozen active ingredient is *the user bounds the
inference to what the evidence supports* — Option A is the only one where the **user performs** the sort (B
narrows *for* them; C is more cognitive-abstract). A carries the lowest positive-thinking risk (it's about what
the event *establishes/can't prove*, not reframing to flattery), is consistent with the Read & Decide sorter
(strengthens the universal shell), and is mobile/accessible via tap-to-assign. **B's dimension labels**
(*is this about everyone or this person? forever or this time? who you are or what happened?*) are used as
*sorting hints*, and **C's question** is an optional "still convinced?" nudge — augmenting fidelity without
choosing a prettier-but-more-passive interaction.

## DELIVERABLE 8 & 9 — Full Production T1a: Screen-by-Screen UX + Consumer Copy
*(Consumer never sees "bound the inference," T1a, PE-1, etc.)*

**S-WM-1 · Recognition gate**
> **Does this happen for you?**
> *"When something doesn't work out with someone, it can start to feel like it means something bigger about me
> — like there's something wrong with me, or this is just how it'll always go."*
> **[ Yes, this happens ]** · **[ Not really me ]** → board · **[ I handle this okay ]** → healthy-marker + board.

**S-WM-2 · The Shift**
> When a date or a relationship doesn't work out, your mind can do something quick and unfair: it takes *one
> thing that happened* and turns it into *a verdict about you.* One person's choice becomes "I'm not enough."
> A few disappointments become "this always happens because something's wrong with me."
> This Play helps you do one specific thing: **keep the conclusion the size of the actual evidence** — so a
> hard moment stays a hard moment, instead of becoming a definition of you. It won't make the sting go away.
> It just stops the sting from writing a story it doesn't have the evidence for.
> **[ Show me how ]**

**S-WM-3 · Why this matters (Level-1 — Deliverable 18)**
> Getting turned down, or watching something fade, genuinely hurts — that's not a flaw, that's being a person
> who cares. But there's a step that happens almost automatically: a specific event ("this didn't work out
> with this person") quietly expands into a global claim ("I'm unlovable," "I'll always be alone"). The claim
> feels true because it's painful — but pain isn't evidence.
> This Play isn't about thinking positive, and it's absolutely not about pretending you don't care. It's about
> one honest question: *what does this actually prove — and what does it not?* Kept to its real size, a
> rejection is information about one situation. Blown up, it becomes a story about your whole worth that the
> evidence never supported.
> You'll still be allowed to feel hurt. You'll just stop handing a small event a much bigger job than it can do.
> **[ Understand this deeper ▸ ]** *(L2 — Deliverable 19)*

**S-WM-4 · Learn the move**
> When a letdown starts becoming a verdict, run it through three questions:
> - **What does this actually establish?** (the narrow, true thing that happened)
> - **What does it *not* establish?** (the big claims it can't prove)
> - **What's the narrowest true thing I can say?**
> The trick is separating *this happened* from *this is who I am.* Let's try it on someone else's situation
> first.
> **[ Try one ]**

**S-WM-5 · Do-It-With-Me — Scenario A (clear rejection)** *(signature interaction)*
> **The situation:** *"After a few good dates, they said: 'I had a really good time, but I don't think we're
> the right match.'"* And the thought that showed up: *"Something must be wrong with me."*
> Here's what that moment can be made to mean. Sort each one:
> **[ This event supports this ]** vs **[ This event can't prove this ]**
> - "This person didn't want to keep dating me." → *supports*
> - "We weren't the right match for *them*." → *supports*
> - "I'm not enough." → *can't prove*
> - "No one will ever choose me." → *can't prove*
> - "There's something wrong with me." → *can't prove*
> *(Optional hint on each: is this about **this person** or **everyone**? **this time** or **forever**? **what
> happened** or **who you are**?)*
> After sorting → **Build the narrowest true thing:** *(sentence builder, prefilled from the "supports"
> column)* → e.g. **"This person didn't want to keep dating me, and that's disappointing."**
> *(If a user drops "I'm not enough" into **supports**: gentle text — "That's the story, not the evidence. What
> did the event actually show? That this one person didn't want to continue. The rest is the leap we're
> catching.")*

**S-WM-6 · Do-It-With-Me — Scenario B (ghosting)**
> **The situation:** *"Someone you were really enjoying just… stopped replying. No explanation."* Thought:
> *"I'm forgettable. Nobody ever stays interested."*
> **Important:** this one's frustrating, and going quiet on someone isn't a great thing to do. We're **not**
> saying you shouldn't be bothered. We're only checking the *global* conclusion.
> Sort: "They stopped responding" *(supports)* · "It was rude / it stung" *(supports — your reaction is valid)*
> · "I'm forgettable" *(can't prove)* · "Nobody ever stays interested" *(can't prove — that's 'always/nobody'
> language)*.
> Narrowest true thing → *"This person stopped responding, and that was frustrating."*

**S-WM-7 · Do-It-With-Me — Scenario C (repeated pattern → pattern branch)**
> **The situation:** *"The last three people you were into slowly became less available."* Thought: *"This keeps
> happening because I'm fundamentally unlovable."*
> This one's different — there might be a **real pattern** here, and we're not going to tell you it's imaginary.
> Two things are getting tangled:
> - **The pattern** — "the last three people I pursued became less available." *That's an observation. It might
>   be worth a closer look.*
> - **The verdict** — "because I'm fundamentally unlovable." *That's a global cause the pattern doesn't prove.*
> Sort them, then: keep the observation, drop the verdict. **Narrowest true thing** → *"The last three people I
> pursued became less available — and I don't yet know why."*
> **[ That pattern's worth looking at → Read It, Then Decide ]** *(routes the recurrence into Read & Decide as
> evidence — not a self-verdict).* We won't guess the reason for you.

**S-WM-8 · Your Turn (own event → produces the output)** — Deliverable 11
> Now one of yours. Keep it to one specific thing — not a whole era.
> 1. **What happened?** *(one specific event — short field)*
> 2. **What did you turn it into?** *(the conclusion about you — short field or pick a common one)*
> 3. **What does it actually establish?** *(add the narrow true things — chips)*
> 4. **What does it *not* establish?** *(add the big claims it can't prove — chips; suggestions offered:
>    "about everyone," "forever," "who I am")*
> 5. **The narrowest true thing:** *(sentence builder from step 3)*

**S-WM-9 · And this can still hurt** *(emotion beat — not the target)*
> One last thing. Bounding the conclusion doesn't mean you're fine, or that you didn't want it, or that it
> didn't matter. You can hold both:
> **[ your narrowest true thing ]** — *and this can still hurt.*
> You just did the hard part: you kept the fact and dropped the verdict.

**S-WM-10 · Your Bounded Conclusion (Executable Output)** — Deliverable 12
> **What you're taking from this:** *"[the narrowest true thing] — and this can still hurt."*
> **[ Take it with you ]** · **[ Save to My Plays ]**

**S-WM-11 · Take It With You (Portable)** — Deliverable 14
> Next time a letdown starts becoming a verdict, three questions — no app needed:
> 1. **What does this actually establish?**
> 2. **What does it *not* establish?**
> 3. **What's the narrowest true thing?**

**S-WM-12 · Use It in Real Life**
> **Use this when:** a dating letdown starts turning into a sentence about *you* — "what's wrong with me," "this
> always happens," "I'm not enough."
> **Do:** run the three questions before the story hardens. Keep the fact, drop the verdict, keep the feeling.
> **If it's bigger than a dating moment** — a belief about yourself that follows you everywhere, not just after
> a date — see the note below. *(→ S-WM-15 signpost)*

**S-WM-13 · I Used This → Return & Review (fidelity)** — Deliverable 15
> **[ I used this in real life ]** →
> - What happened? *(short)*
> - What conclusion showed up? *(short)*
> - Be honest — which one? **[ I bounded it — kept the fact, dropped the verdict ]** *(reinforce)* / **[ I
>   caught it but still believed the big story ]** *(re-coach: catching it is the first rep; try writing the
>   narrowest true thing)* / **[ I flipped it into "their loss / I'm amazing" ]** *(re-coach: that's the
>   opposite spin — aim for true, not flattering)* / **[ I noticed there might be a real pattern ]** *(→ offer
>   Read It, Then Decide)*
> - Keep or update your bounded conclusion? **[ Keep ] / [ Update ]**
> **No points, no streak.** Still feeling sad or disappointed is normal — that's not the technique failing.

**S-WM-14 · My Plays card** — Deliverable 16

> - **WHEN THIS COMES UP:** a dating letdown starts becoming a statement about me

## DELIVERABLE 13 — T1a Fidelity Feedback
- **Correct use:** the conclusion stays close to the actual event; doesn't expand one choice into a universal
  truth; doesn't erase a real observed pattern; **doesn't require feeling better.**
- **Common errors → correction:** *positive spin ("their loss")* → *aim for true, not flattering.* · *emotion
  denial ("I shouldn't care")* → *you can care and still bound the conclusion.* · *pattern denial ("it's all in
  my head")* → *if a pattern is observable, keep it and look at it (that's Read & Decide).* · *global causal
  leap ("this keeps happening because I'm broken")* → *the recurrence may be real; the global self-cause isn't
  proven.* · *partner diagnosis ("they're avoidant/a narcissist")* → *bounded inference applies to conclusions
  about them too — stay with what the evidence supports.*
- **Never uses relationship outcome as proof of correct use.**

## DELIVERABLE 19 — T1a Level-2 Literature ("Understand this deeper") *(draft)*
> **What's happening.** When a painful event hits, the mind tends to explain it in a *global, stable, internal*
> way — "this is about all of me, it'll always be like this, it's my fault" — rather than a *specific, temporary*
> one. Personally painful events widen into identity conclusions especially fast. Naming the event from a small
> step back ("what does this establish?") tends to produce clearer, less distorted reasoning than staying
> inside the spiral.
> **What it changes / doesn't.** It doesn't make anyone want you, and it doesn't remove the hurt. It changes
> the *size of the conclusion* you draw — which changes what you do next (keep dating as yourself vs. carrying
> "I'm defective" into the next conversation).
> **Pattern vs. verdict.** A real recurring pattern is information worth examining — keep it. "I'm fundamentally
> unlovable" is a global verdict a pattern doesn't establish. We separate the two and send the pattern to a
> place built to look at evidence (Read It, Then Decide).
> **When it's not enough.** If the self-belief is chronic, follows you everywhere, or is tied to depression or
> trauma, this dating tool isn't the right container — a mental health professional is.
> **Evidence status (honest).** The building blocks draw on established psychology (how people explain painful
> events; the reasoning benefits of a small amount of self-distance). **Using them to keep dating letdowns from
> becoming self-verdicts is a reasoned application we believe in but haven't formally tested — we won't pretend
> it's proven.** *(Internal source notes: attribution/global-vs-specific literature; Grossmann & Kross 2014
> self-distanced reasoning — verify at content-final.)*

# FINAL QUESTIONS
**1 · T1a actionable or drifted to journaling?** **Actionable** — the user *sorts* claims (supports / can't
prove) and *builds* a bounded sentence; no "how did that feel?" boxes.
**2 · What does the user DO?** Sorts what an event establishes vs can't prove, then writes the narrowest true
thing (and, for patterns, separates recurrence from verdict).
**3 · Executable output?** A **bounded conclusion** ("[narrowest true thing] — and this can still hurt").
**4 · What can they do differently after?** Catch a letdown becoming a self-verdict and keep the conclusion the
size of the evidence — carrying the fact, not "I'm defective," into the next situation.
**5 · Usable without the app?** **Yes** — three portable questions (establishes / doesn't establish / narrowest
true thing).
**6 · Preserves rejection pain while reducing global inference?** **Yes** — the "And this can still hurt" beat +
success explicitly not defined by feeling better.
**7 · Accidentally positive thinking?** **No** — the target is *true, not flattering*; "their loss" is a
flagged error with correction.
**8 · Accidentally therapy?** **No** — situational-only gate + support signpost for chronic/severe; no
diagnosis, no trauma processing.
**9 · Preserves real repeated patterns as evidence?** **Yes** — pattern branch keeps the recurrence and routes
it to Read & Decide; the global self-cause is the only thing dropped.
**10 · Engaging enough for a digital product?** **Yes** — active sorting + sentence building + optional
dimension hints; not a wall of text.
**11 · As actionable as Read & Decide despite being interpretive?** **Yes** — the operation is externalized
into a sort + build (Deliverable 22).
**12 · Does the universal container support both?** **Yes** — with **optional slots** (emotion-beat, safety,
two-stage) and a shared sort engine; not identical rigid screens.
**13 · Universal in code?** Container/shell, sort engine, recognition gate, literature slots, output→portable→
My Plays, used→review, state model, safety slot (Deliverable 24).
**14 · Technique-specific?** Bucket labels + sort content, feedback/accuracy logic, if-then builder (R&D),
emotion beat + pattern-route (T1a), scenario content.
**15 · Still worth $29 with two Plays?** **Yes, more so** — spans interpretive *and* behavioral change; the
hardest translation (interpretive-without-journaling) works.
**16 · Weakest MVP Play now?** By coverage/evidence, the **PE-6 gap** (no T2d in MVP) is the weak spot; among
built Plays, **How Much to Put In (T2c)** carries the most misuse risk (scorekeeping) and needs the tightest
guardrail copy.
**17 · Remaining design blockers?** **None hard** — remaining items (reading-level/safety-copy pass, PE-6/T2d
timing, scenario replay depth, sort-engine screen-reader QA) are copy-final/product tasks, not architecture
gaps.
**18 · Ready for technical implementation planning?** **READY FOR TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION PLANNING.** Two
Plays are screen-complete; the universal container + shared sort engine + slot model give a clear build
architecture; no product/therapeutic/interaction logic must be invented at build time. Standing limiter
