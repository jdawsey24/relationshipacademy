# DELIVERABLE 4 — Production-Quality Playbook Opening *(draft copy)*
**S-OPEN-1 · Your result**
> **Your result: Difficulty Feeling Chosen**
> Wanting to be chosen is human. Everyone wants to feel wanted — that's not the problem.
>
> This result is about something quieter. It's what happens when the question *"Do they want me?"* starts
> running the show — shaping how you read what happens, how you see yourself, how you show up, and what you
> decide — often before you've asked whether *this person* is even right for *you*.
>
> That's the part worth working on. Not so you'll be easier to choose. So you can see clearly — and choose too,
> instead of waiting to be picked.
>
> This shows up differently for different people, and you won't work on all of it. Next, we'll help you spot
> the parts that sound like you — and you pick where to start.

# DELIVERABLE 11 — FULL PRODUCTION PROTOTYPE: **"Read It, Then Decide"** (T2a→T2b)
*Production-quality copy + screen-by-screen UX. Consumer never sees T2a/T2b/discrimination/implementation-
intention.*

### S-RD-1 · Recognition gate
> **Does this happen for you?**
> *"I can't always tell what their behavior actually means — or when to keep going, ask, slow down, or walk
> away."*
> **[ Yes, this happens ]** → continue · **[ Not really me ]** → back to board · **[ I handle this okay ]** →
> healthy-marker + back to board.
- Interaction: 3 buttons. Data: gate response. State: Available→Explored on "Yes." A11y: buttons, focus order.
  Mobile: full-width stacked.

### S-RD-2 · The Shift
> Right now, an unclear signal can turn into a whole story fast — and the story decides what you do. *They took
> a while to reply → "they're losing interest" → you pull back (or push harder).*
> This helps you do something different: tell what you've **actually seen** from what you're **guessing**,
> notice what you still **don't know**, and set a **clear next move** — so your decision follows the evidence,
> not the fear or the hope.
> **[ Show me how ]**

### S-RD-3 · Why this matters (Level-1 literature, ~150 words) — Deliverable 12
> Early dating is genuinely unclear. You're working with small, mixed signals, and your mind fills the gaps —
> usually with whatever you're most afraid of or most hoping for. That's normal. But when a guess gets treated
> like a fact, you end up reacting to a story instead of the person.
>
> Two things quietly help. First, keeping *what happened* separate from *what you think it means* — because
> those get blurred fast. Second, deciding in advance what you'd need to actually see before you change how much
> you're putting in. That turns "I'll just wait and hope" into a clear, self-respecting plan.
>
> This isn't about being suspicious or keeping score. It's about giving yourself accurate information to work
> with — so your choices are yours, not just reactions.
> **[ Understand this deeper ▸ ]** *(expands L2 — Deliverable 13)*

### S-RD-4 · Learn the move
> Three buckets. When something happens and you feel a story forming, sort it:
> - **Saw it** — what actually happened (their words, actions, timing).
> - **Guessing** — what you think it means. (Could be right. Still a guess.)
> - **Don't know yet** — what this doesn't tell you.
> Then one question: **what would actually tell me?** — the next real piece of information.
> **[ Try it with an example ]**

### S-RD-5 · Do-It-With-Me — Scenario 1 (sorting) — Deliverable 14
> **The situation:** *"You had a great date — easy conversation, they said they'd love to do it again. Over the
> next four days their texts got shorter. Then they messaged suggesting a specific plan for next week."*
> Drag each piece into a bucket (or tap to place):
> - "The date went well and they said they'd like to see me again" → *(Saw it)*
> - "Their texts got shorter over four days" → *(Saw it)*
> - "They're losing interest" → *(Guessing)*
> - "They suggested a specific plan for next week" → *(Saw it)*
> - "Whether the shorter texts are about me, their week, or just how they text" → *(Don't know yet)*
- Interaction: tap-to-assign (drag optional). **If "they're losing interest" is placed in *Saw it*:** gentle
  correction — *"That's a guess, not something you saw. Their shorter texts are what you saw — what it means is
  still open."* (This is the one place with an accuracy criterion; elsewhere no right/wrong.)
> System note after sort: *"Notice: the only 'losing interest' evidence is shorter texts — and they just made a
> concrete plan. The story ran ahead of what you actually saw."*
> **What would actually tell you?** → **[ How they are on the plan they suggested ]** · [ Something else ]

### S-RD-6 · Do-It-With-Me — Scenario 2 (choose the evidence question)
> *"They keep saying they really like you — but they dodge making actual plans."*
> Sort: says they like me *(Saw it — a word)* · avoids plans *(Saw it — an action)* · "they're scared but into
> me" *(Guessing)* · whether words or actions are the better signal here *(the real question)*.
> **What would tell you most?** → **[ Whether they follow through on a plan, not whether they say nice things ]**
> Teaching beat: *when words and actions disagree, actions are usually the clearer signal — but you're
> gathering information, not delivering a verdict.*

### S-RD-7 · Your Turn (produces the executable output — Stage 1)
> Now a real situation of yours.
> - **What's the question you actually want answered?** *(short text — e.g., "Is this going somewhere or am I
>   filling in the blanks?")*
> - **Saw it:** *(add items)* · **Guessing:** *(add items)* · **Don't know yet:** *(add items)*
> - **What would actually tell you?** *(one thing)*
- Interaction: three add-lists + two short-text fields. Not a journal — short chips, not paragraphs.
> **Sufficiency check:** *"Do you already have enough to answer your question?"* **[ I think I do → decide ]** /
> **[ Not yet → I'll watch for that one thing ]**

### S-RD-8 · Stage 2 unlock — Set your move (T2b)
> You've read the situation. Now turn it into a move — decided *now*, so you're not deciding in the moment from
> fear or hope.
> **If** *(the thing you'll watch for)* **→ then I will** *(pick one):*
> - keep watching a little longer
> - ask directly / get clarity
> - keep my investment where it is
> - put in a bit more
> - pull back a little
> - step away
> - revisit this in a bit
> **Quick check:** this is about *your move in response to what you see* — not a way to make them do something,
> and not a deadline. **[ Looks right ]** / **[ Let me rework it ]**

### S-RD-9 · Your Read & Decide (Executable Output — Deliverable 15)
> **Here's what you've got:**
> - **The question:** [their text]
> - **What you saw / what you're guessing / what's still open:** [the map]
> - **What would tell you:** [evidence]
> - **Your move:** If [condition] → then I'll [action]
> **[ Take it with you ]** · **[ Save to My Plays ]**

### S-RD-10 · Take It With You (Portable — Deliverable 16)
> Next time it's unclear, run this in your head — no app needed:
> 1. **What did I actually see?**
> 2. **What am I guessing?**
> 3. **What don't I know yet?**
> 4. **What would tell me?**
> 5. **If I see ___, I'll ___.**

### S-RD-11 · Use It in Real Life
> **Use this when:** a text or a change in how they're acting starts turning into a story — or when you're
> unsure whether to keep going, ask, ease off, or walk away.
> **Do:** run the five lines before you react. Then watch for the one thing that would actually tell you.
> **Heads up:** if what you're "waiting to be sure about" is whether someone is treating you badly or unsafely —
> you don't need more evidence for that. Trust it. *(safety signpost — Deliverable 28)*

### S-RD-12 · I Used This → Return & Review (Deliverable 17) — fidelity-focused
> **[ I used this in real life ]** opens:
> - What came up? *(short)*
> - Which part did you use? *(chips: read the situation / set a move / both)*
> - What became clearer? *(short)*
> - Be honest — did you… *(pick one)* **[ separate what you saw from what you guessed ]** *(on-track)* /
>   **[ end up treating a guess as a fact ]** *(→ gentle re-coach)* / **[ set a move about your response ]**
>   *(on-track)* / **[ set a deadline or a way to make them act ]** *(→ re-coach)*
> - Does your move still fit, or update it? **[ Keep ] / [ Update ]**
> - What's next?
> **No points. No streak.** A date ending is **not** a failure here — reading clearly sometimes means seeing
> it's not a fit. *(Deliverable 40 / outcome≠success)*

### S-RD-13 · My Plays card (Deliverable 19/22)
> **Read It, Then Decide**
> - **WHEN THIS COMES UP:** a signal turns into a story, or I'm stuck on whether to keep going
> - **MY MOVE:** saw it / guessing / don't know / what would tell me → if ___, then ___
> - **WHAT I'M LOOKING FOR:** the one thing that would actually answer my question
> - **WATCH OUT FOR:** treating a guess as a fact; making it a deadline or a test of them
> - **REMEMBER:** decide from what I see, not from fear or hope

**Fidelity feedback (Deliverable 18/21) for this Play:**
- **Correct use looks like:** kept *saw it* separate from *guessing*; named a real *don't-know*; the move has an
  **observable** trigger + **your** action.
- **Common misapplication:** filing clear, repeated behavior under "don't know" to avoid a conclusion; a rule
  that's a countdown or a way to make them chase.
- **This Play does NOT mean:** playing it cool, keeping score, testing them, or needing 100% certainty before
  you act.

**Error/empty states:** empty add-lists → *"Add at least one thing you actually saw."* · no move set →
*"You can save just the read now and set a move later."* · connection loss → outputs saved locally, *"We kept
your work."*
**Accessibility (this Play):** sorting is **tap-to-assign with a labeled bucket selector** (drag is optional
enhancement); each item is a button announcing its current bucket; correction messages are text (not color);
focus moves to the correction; reduced-motion removes the sort animation. **Mobile:** buckets stack vertically;
add-lists use a single-line input + "add" chip; the if-then builder is two dropdowns + one field, one-handed.

# DELIVERABLE 12 — Level-1 Literature for All MVP Plays *(drafts, ~100–200 words each; only Read & Decide shown in full above; others summarized)*
- **What It Actually Means (T1a):** one outcome is one data point about one situation — not a verdict on you.
  When it becomes "something's wrong with me," you're reacting to a conclusion the evidence doesn't support.
  This keeps the hurt (it's real) but bounds the conclusion to what actually happened. *(~120 words in build)*
- **Is This Right for You? (T1c):** it's easy to spend a date auditioning — reading their face for approval —
  and never ask whether *you* even like how this feels. This gives you your own things to watch for and treats
  your own reactions as information. *(~120)*
- **How Much to Put In (T2c):** it's natural to keep investing because you already have — but what you've
  already given isn't a reason to give more. This helps you set your next level of effort from what you're
  actually seeing, and leave enough room to *see* what they bring. Not a game, not keeping score. *(~150,
  carries the anti-scorekeeping guardrail)*
- **Say the Real Thing (T3a):** editing yourself to stay likable means people are choosing a version of you —
  so you can't tell if *you* fit. This is a small, safe experiment: predict, say one real thing, watch what
  happens. Their response is information about fit, not a grade on your worth. *(~150, safety-aware)*
- **Rest, or Giving Up? (T4a):** dating fatigue is real, and rest is a legitimate choice. The issue isn't
  resting — it's drifting out without deciding, because discouragement made the call. This helps you choose on
  purpose, with a sign that tells you when you're ready. *(~130)*

# DELIVERABLE 13 — Level-2 Literature for Prototype ("Understand this deeper") *(draft)*
> **What's happening.** Under uncertainty, people fill missing information with expectation — often shaped by
> past disappointment (this is well-documented in interpersonal research on how anxious expectations bias what
> we perceive). The move here is to slow that automatic step: separate observation from inference, name what's
> genuinely unknown, and pre-decide what evidence would change your investment.
> **What it changes.** It doesn't make anyone more interested. It changes *what you're reacting to* — evidence
> instead of a story — and turns open-ended waiting into a decision you can respect.
> **What it does not mean.** Not suspicion, not keeping score, not a purity test, not certainty before acting.
> "I need more information" is legitimate — but it shouldn't become forever.
> **When it doesn't apply.** If the "unclear thing" is whether someone is mistreating or endangering you, you
> don't need more evidence — act on it.
> **Evidence status (honest).** The *pieces* draw on established psychology — pre-committing to "if-then" plans
> reliably helps people act on their intentions (implementation-intentions research; Gollwitzer & Sheeran,
> 2006), and separating observation from inference is a core reasoning skill. **Applying them to dating
> decisions is a reasoned extension we believe in but have not yet formally tested.** We won't pretend
> otherwise. *(Internal source note: Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006; interpretation-bias/rejection-sensitivity
> literature — verify at content-final.)*

# DELIVERABLE 24 — Accessibility Specification
Keyboard-complete (all sorts/builders operable without a mouse); screen-reader semantics (each bucket/list/
chip labeled with role + current value); visible focus states; contrast ≥ WCAG AA; **no info by color alone**
(states use label+icon; corrections are text); reduced-motion honored (transitions removed, not just shortened);
touch targets ≥44px; plain-language errors; **every drag interaction has a tap-to-assign / dropdown
alternative** (Read & Decide sort is tap-first, drag-optional); focus moves to feedback/corrections; no
time-pressure anywhere.

# DELIVERABLE 25 — Mobile UX Specification
Mobile-first: single-column; buckets/cards stack; if-then builder = 2 dropdowns + 1 field; add-lists = single-
line input + "add" chip (minimal keyboard burden); sticky "save/continue"; save-and-return persists partial
outputs; modals avoided (use full screens); progress shown as light step dots (not a course bar); one-handed
reachability for primary CTAs; no desktop-hover-dependent interactions.

# DELIVERABLE 27 — Multi-Problem User Journey (recognizes 4–5)
Taps: self-meaning (T1a), ambiguous evidence (R&D), over-investment (T2c), self-editing (T3a), + loneliness
(RC-01). Board shows **4 Play cards** + a loneliness validation note (not a card). Framing: *"Start with
whichever one pulls at you — you don't need to do all of these."* **No forced order** (no prerequisites across
these). Conditional T2c appears because it was recognized. User starts R&D, saves it, comes back another day
for T3a. **Not overwhelming** because: 4 cards (not 6), one-area start encouraged, conditional-only-when-
recognized, loneliness handled as validation. Priority is **user-chosen**, lightly hinted ("many people find
[Read & Decide] a useful anchor") — not imposed.

# DELIVERABLE 28 — Low-Match User Journey (mostly loneliness + one pathway)
Taps only: "tired of being alone" (RC-01) + "worn out by dating" (T4a). Board shows **one Play** (Rest, or
Giving Up?) + a warm loneliness validation panel. The Playbook **does not manufacture** more Plays. Experience:
honest validation of loneliness (*not a problem to fix*), one genuinely relevant Play, optional "explore
another area" if they're curious, contextual literature. **Validity test PASS:** a low-match user gets an
honest, useful, non-pathologizing experience — and the product does **not** invent pathology to justify $29.
*(If loneliness were pushing decisions, the RC-01 copy routes them to Read & Decide — but only on first-party
recognition.)*

# DELIVERABLE 29 — "I Already Do This" Journey
At any recognition prompt or Play gate, **[ I handle this okay ]** → a brief affirming healthy-marker (*"Nice —
that's exactly the move: you already separate what happened from what it says about you. Keep it."*), no Play
forced, return to board. The Playbook **recognizes existing strengths** and never pathologizes competence.

# DELIVERABLE 30 — Consumer Comprehension Audit
Opening/recognition/Read & Decide copy: no jargon; ~5th–7th grade; each screen answers *what's happening / why
it matters / what to do / when / what not to do.* The one place needing care is the sort's accuracy correction
(kept to one plain sentence). **PASS**, pending a real reading-level pass at copy-final.

# DELIVERABLE 31 — Actionability Audit
Every Read & Decide screen has a **user action** (tap/sort/build/decide), not just read: S-RD-4 learn → S-RD-5/6
sort → S-RD-7 build own map → S-RD-8 build rule → outputs. The only read-only screens (S-RD-2/3) orient before
action and are short/optional-deepenable. **PASS.**

# DELIVERABLE 32 — Generic-Advice Audit
Could this be an Instagram carousel? **No.** "Pay attention to actions not words" (generic) → here it's
*operationalized*: sort saw-it/guessing/don't-know, name the informative next evidence, pre-set an if-then move.
The user **produces** an evidence map + a decision rule. **PASS.** (Watch: T4a and T2c L1 copy must stay
operational, not slide into "take a break / don't chase" clichés.)

# DELIVERABLE 33 — Outcome-vs-Technique-Success Audit
Read & Decide success = *you separated observation from inference, named a real unknown, and set an evidence-
based move* — explicitly **not** getting a text back or staying together. S-RD-12 states a date ending isn't
failure. **PASS** across Plays (each Play's Return & Review avoids outcome as the metric).

# DELIVERABLE 34 — $29 Consumer Value Audit (critical)
**Delivers:** personalized to what the user recognized; genuinely interactive (you *do* the operation);
leaves reusable tools (portable forms + My Plays); differentiated from Snapshot/advice/journal. **Weaknesses:**
(1) value hinges on real-world use we can't force; (2) two read-heavy orienting screens per Play risk feeling
like "content" if copy isn't tight; (3) MVP without T2d leaves the large PE-6 group with recognition/validation
but no dedicated intervention (mitigated by RC-02 + Read & Decide routing, but a felt gap for some); (4) honest
"no guaranteed outcome" is less seductive than competitors. **Net: worth $29 if the interactions stay
do-it-with-me and copy stays tight; at risk if it drifts toward reading.**

# FINAL QUESTIONS
**1 · Opening clarifies the Playbook's purpose?** **Yes** — result + reframe (the problem is when "do they
want me?" runs the show) + agency (you won't work on all of it) in one screen.
**2 · Recognition feels like personalization, not another assessment?** **Yes** — a short tap-pass of lived
lines, no scoring/quiz; the user recognizes themselves rather than being tested.
**3 · Can users understand why Plays surfaced without feeling diagnosed?** **Yes** — *"based on what sounded
familiar, you may want to start here,"* never "we detected that you…".
**4 · First prototype: T1a or Read & Decide?** **Read & Decide** (most interactive, most concrete output,
best proves "you do something"); T1a is prototype #2.
**5 · Teaches a technique, not advice?** **Yes** — the user sorts evidence and builds an if-then move; that's
an operation, not a slogan.
**6 · Can the user perform the technique inside the product?** **Yes** — do-it-with-me scenarios then their own
situation.
**7 · Leaves with an executable output?** **Yes** — evidence map + evidence question + if-then move.
**8 · Usable without reopening the Playbook?** **Yes** — the five-line portable form (→ My Plays).
**9 · "Used in real life" creates return value?** **Yes** — opens a fidelity-focused review that can update the
saved move; no points.
**10 · My Plays worth returning to?** **Yes** — concise behavioral reference, editable, no journaling.
**11 · Too much reading?** **Low risk** — two short orienting screens per Play; everything else is action;
must be watched at copy-final.
**12 · Too much reflection?** **No** — outputs are structured, not free-text; open text is minimal and
supportive.
**13 · Any interaction accidentally therapy?** **No** — bounded techniques, R3 signposts for entrenched/
severe, no trauma processing or partner diagnosis.
**14 · Any unsupported RLC theory introduced?** **No** — everything traces to the frozen architecture; no new
construct was needed for a screen.
**15 · Still worth $29 after seeing the real experience?** **Qualified yes** — if it stays do-it-with-me and
copy stays tight; genuinely personalized and reusable (weaknesses in Deliverable 34).
**16 · Weakest part?** The two orienting read screens per Play (drift-to-content risk) and MVP's thin PE-6
(distorted-expectancy) coverage without T2d.
**17 · Remove?** Any orienting copy that repeats the reframe; avoid a second scenario if one teaches the sort
adequately for lower-effort users.
**18 · Strengthen?** Keep the do-it-with-me density high; tighten L1 copy; add the reading-level/safety pass;
consider accelerating T2d if PE-6 users feel under-served.
**19 · Ready for implementation?** **READY FOR IMPLEMENTATION** — the Read & Decide prototype is specified to
the screen (copy, interactions, data, outputs, fidelity, states, a11y, mobile, errors) so implementation
translates a spec rather than inventing product logic. **No hard blockers.** The Deliverable-35 items are
copy-final and product-choice tasks (My Plays labels, priority hinting, reading-level pass), resolvable during
implementation prep, not architecture gaps. Standing limiter (efficacy theory-derived/untested) carried openly
in-product (L2 copy).
